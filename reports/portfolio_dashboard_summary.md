# ProductPulse: from raw ecommerce events to a defensible dashboard model

**Business problem.** Ecommerce stakeholders need to understand where product interest fails to progress to purchase, whether observed behavior differs by device or day, and whether data quality supports those comparisons. A convincing chart needs consistent session definitions and traceable denominators before it can support a business discussion.

**Scope and methods.** ProductPulse analyzed the real public, obfuscated GA4 ecommerce sample for January 1â€“7, 2021. Bounded BigQuery queries and dry runs controlled scans. December 31 and January 8 supplied context. The pipeline keeps one composite user/session identity across midnight, requires strict view â†’ cart â†’ checkout â†’ purchase timestamp order, and assigns device from the earliest observed session event with deterministic ties. Python calculations were reconciled against independent BigQuery context aggregates and DuckDB session/daily SQL. Synthetic fixtures remain separate from the real-data package.

**Observed results.** The calendar window contains 245,209 events, 22,847 distinct pseudonyms and 26,331 active eligible sessions. The observed-entry cohort contains 26,328 sessions. Its ordered funnel is 3,942 viewing sessions â†’ 743 cart sessions â†’ 190 checkout sessions â†’ 91 purchase sessions: **2.31% view-to-purchase conversion**. The largest observed step drop is view to cart, **81.15%**. Device conversion is desktop **2.20%**, mobile **2.38%**, and tablet **3.74%**, with only **4** ordered tablet purchases. These are descriptive comparisons, not evidence of a device effect or a causal UX problem.

**Trust and limitations.** Source attribution is missing on 26.76% of calendar events and medium on 14.67%; these are event-weighted first-user acquisition fields. Four cohort sessions touch outer context and remain included and flagged. Excluding them as a sensitivity check yields 2.28% conversion, without changing the primary definition. The sample is obfuscated, pseudonyms are not people, a week is a short observation period, and finite context cannot establish complete customer journeys. There are no supported revenue, retention, experiment or causal claims.

**Dashboard preparation.** The deliverable includes audited CSV schemas, explicit denominators, a field-level data dictionary, package-local surrogate keys, date-filterable distinct-count presence tables, typed Power Query expressions, proposed DAX measures and a three-page report specification. Single-direction dimension relationships and disconnected audit aggregates prevent accidental many-to-many inflation. The date slicer has explicit calendar-activity and cohort-entry meanings; device filters apply only to the cohort. Local automated checks validate exports, tampering failures and filter semantics.

**Implementation status.** This work prepares a Power BI report; it does not claim a finished dashboard. No `.pbix` exists or has been verified in this phase. Proposed DAX, Power Query expressions, theme import, visual rendering and interactions require manual Power BI Desktop acceptance. No new BigQuery queries or cloud-setting changes were needed for dashboard preparation.

**Interview discussion.** For Business Analyst roles, explain how a checkout question becomes a metric contract and acceptance criteria. For Data Analyst roles, demonstrate composite keys, distinct-count pitfalls, rate denominators, source reconciliation and boundary sensitivity. For Product Analyst roles, distinguish observed funnel loss from evidence of a cause, discuss attribution gaps and small device samples, and explain why additional evidence is needed before recommending an intervention. A reasonable next investigation is to inspect instrumentation and product-view-to-cart paths, not to claim a tested uplift.
